# Competitor and Content Landscape: Creators Reaching Millennial Women with ADHD and Millennial Mothers

Research date: 2026-10-26. Evidence window prioritized: last 12 months, with the last 90 days (since roughly 2026-07-28) flagged where visible.

## Read-me: method, legends, and the most important limitation

**Most important limitation (read first).** Only WebSearch was usable (no page fetching). 26 searches ran successfully. The two searches aimed at TikTok captions (`ADHD mom` and `ADHD women AI ChatGPT tips`, both restricted to tiktok.com) were refused because the session-wide search budget ran out. No Instagram, YouTube or TikTok profile or caption searches succeeded. Everything below therefore comes from journalism, podcast directories, publisher pages, Substack URLs, research abstracts and press coverage, **not** from platform-native captions, comment threads or follower counters. Several requested creators and topics could not be searched at all (see "Gaps and limitations").

**Quote fidelity labels**
- TITLE/CAPTION: the text appeared as a search result title.
- SUMMARY-QUOTED: the text appeared inside quotation marks in the search tool's summary.
- SUMMARY-TEXT (unmarked): the words appeared verbatim in the search tool's summary but without quotation marks (for example, bolded episode titles). Treat as lower fidelity than SUMMARY-QUOTED.
- URL-SLUG: the wording appears only in the URL path. Reproduced exactly as the slug, not reconstructed into a title.
- Punctuation note: the brief bans em dashes, so where an original title contained a long dash it is shown here as " - " and flagged "(dash normalized)".

**Source type labels:** FIRSTHAND (creator's own page or show), RESEARCH (peer-reviewed or academic), JOURNALISM, EXPERT/CREATOR OPINION, COMMERCIAL (publisher, retailer, agency, influencer marketplace, or creator sales page). INTERPRETATION marks my own analysis.

**Number provenance labels:** "reported by [publication], [date]" or "as shown in search output, unverified". Self-reported numbers from a creator's own page are marked "self-reported".

**Engagement caution (applies throughout):** view counts, downloads and follower numbers are reach signals, not approval signals. The 2025 PLOS One study below found that the most-viewed ADHD TikToks were frequently inaccurate and that heavy viewers rated them more favorably than clinicians did. High engagement here can mean resonance, controversy, misinformation, or all three.

## Repetitive or formulaic content (with evidence)

- **Symptom-list and do-I-have-ADHD content**: the most-viewed ADHD TikToks are dominated by symptom claims, and roughly half are misleading (Yeung 2022: 52/100; Karasavva 2025: under half accurate; Quality in Sport 2026: 52 to 56%). The most common error is normal behavior presented as ADHD. This is the clearest evidence that the "signs you have ADHD" genre is saturated and quality-compromised. RESEARCH.
- **Long-form versions of the same genre exist too** (Carder ep 280 "Do I have ADHD? Breaking Down 10 Symptoms & Common Misconceptions", SUMMARY-QUOTED).
- **Glamorization/trivialization critique** (Live Science URL-SLUG; NCPS "Diagnosed by Socials: ADHD Influencing on Instagram and TikTok", TITLE/CAPTION). JOURNALISM/EXPERT.
- **Formats named in the brief ("POV: ADHD mom" skits, "things I do as an ADHD woman", hack lists; quoted from the brief, not from sources):** I could not retrieve TikTok captions (searches refused), so **I have no direct evidence on the volume or repetitiveness of these specific formats.** Any claim that they are saturated would be INTERPRETATION only.
